About

What We Do

CWRU CHIPS is a student club where we build real semiconductor fab equipment and design actual chips. We're part of the Hacker Fab network, a growing group of university chapters all trying to build working fabs on a student budget. The goal is a MOSFET transistor, built entirely by CWRU undergrads.

Training and Workshops

We run lectures on lithography, thin-film processing, chip design, and semiconductor basics. Everyone does safety training and tool sign-off before touching equipment. You don't need any background, we start from scratch.

Project-Based Learning

We build actual equipment and submit real chip designs. On the fab side we construct tools like UV steppers and spin coaters ourselves. On the chip side we use open-source EDA tools and tape out to silicon through Tiny Tapeout every semester. Everything gets documented and shared publicly.

Community

We've got MSE, ECE, physics, mechanical, and CS students all working together. We bring in guest speakers from industry and research, and we're connected to the global Hacker Fab community. Come to a meeting, hop in the Discord, or shoot us an email. All majors welcome.

Tiny Tapeout

Tiny Tapeout is an open program that lets student teams submit chip designs that get fabricated on real silicon. Each semester we run a design cycle — from learning the tools to submitting a finished design. When the chips come back, we test them on a development board together.

What You'll Actually Do

You learn digital or analog chip design using open-source EDA tools (KiCad, OpenLane, Xschem). You contribute to a real design, review it with the team, and submit it for fabrication on a 130nm process. When the chip arrives, you help test it. The whole cycle happens in one semester.

How to Join This Project

No chip design experience needed. We run internal workshops at the start of each semester. Email us or join the Discord and tell us you want in on the chip design track.

UV Lithography Stepper

We are building a UV lithography stepper based on Carnegie Mellon's open-source design. It uses a repurposed projector lens and UV LED to expose photoresist through a chrome mask at 2 micron resolution. Think[box] funded the build at $2,500.

Why We're Building This

Lithography is the core process of semiconductor manufacturing — you cannot pattern anything without it. This stepper is the foundation of our entire fabrication pipeline. Every other tool we build depends on it working.

How to Get Involved

We need members for mechanical assembly, optical alignment, software, and process testing. No specific skills required — come to a build session and we'll get you started.

Vacuum Spin Coater

We are building a vacuum spin coater that uses a brushless motor and vacuum chuck to spin wafers at controlled speeds, laying down a thin uniform photoresist film before UV exposure.

Why We Need This

Before a wafer can be exposed, it needs an even photoresist coat. An uneven layer ruins the exposure and the whole patterning step fails. This is step zero — without it, the UV stepper cannot work.

How to Get Involved

The build needs people for mechanical design, motor electronics, and firmware. It's a well-scoped, beginner-friendly project — a great first build for new members.

Under construction
Build cost: ~$200 (DIY)

Electroless Metal Plating

We plan to build a wet chemistry station that deposits thin metal films on patterned surfaces through an autocatalytic reaction. No vacuum equipment or high voltages — just chemistry and careful process control.

Why We're Building This

Metal contacts sit on nearly every semiconductor device. Electroless plating lets us add metal to patterned wafers cheaply, and it teaches real surface chemistry and thin-film metallization along the way.

How to Get Involved

This project is in early planning. If you are interested in chemistry, surface science, or materials processing, reach out and we will loop you in as it gets started.

Planning phase
Estimated build: ~$500

Thermal Evaporator

A future build — a vacuum chamber that heats a metal source until it evaporates and condenses on the wafer, depositing a thin, high-purity metal film. We will use this for aluminum and gold contacts on fabricated devices.

Why We Need This

Clean metal contacts are essential for any working transistor. Thermal evaporation produces better films than wet plating and is the standard approach in research fabs. This is what moves us from test structures to real, measurable devices.

How to Get Involved

This build is planned for Year 1–2. Getting involved now means you help plan and lead it. Reach out if you are interested in high-vacuum systems or thin-film engineering.

RF Sputtering System

A future build — an RF-powered vacuum system that ejects atoms from a target to coat the wafer. Unlike thermal evaporation, it can deposit insulators like silicon dioxide. We need this for the gate oxide layer in a MOSFET transistor.

Why We Need This

A MOSFET transistor requires a thin insulating gate oxide between the gate and the channel. Only RF sputtering can deposit this layer. Without it, we cannot complete a transistor — this is the capability that unlocks full device fabrication.

How to Get Involved

This is a longer-term build, planned for Year 2–3. If you are interested in plasma physics, vacuum engineering, or dielectric materials, get in touch now — early planning helps.

Future — Year 2–3
CMU reference build: ~$18,400
